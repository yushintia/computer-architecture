# Week 11 Self-Check Quiz (Ungraded)

Computer Architecture (503872-004). This quiz is **ungraded** — it is
only to help you check what stuck. About 10 minutes. Do not look back at
the slides while you answer.

---

**1.** What is a "cache," in plain words?

A. The computer's entire main memory
B. A small, very fast memory placed close to the processor
C. A backup copy of a hard disk
D. Another name for the operating system

**2.** A processor asks for data and the data is already in the cache.
What is this called?

A. A cache miss
B. A page fault
C. A cache hit
D. A capacity miss

**3.** Which idea explains why a cache can usefully "guess" what data the
processor will need next?

A. Locality
B. Moore's Law
C. The power wall
D. Pipelining

**4.** A cache answers 45 out of 50 memory requests by itself. What is
its hit rate?

A. 45%
B. 90%
C. 5%
D. 50%

**5.** A cache has a hit time of 2 ns, a miss rate of 10%, and a miss
penalty of 80 ns. What is its AMAT?

A. 2 ns
B. 8 ns
C. 10 ns
D. 82 ns

**6.** A program reads a huge dataset for the very first time. Which type
of miss is this?

A. Conflict miss
B. Capacity miss
C. Compulsory miss
D. Hit miss

**7.** Why can a chip not simply make its entire cache huge?

A. Fast memory needs more parts per bit, which costs more money, power, and chip space
B. Large caches are against the law in most countries
C. Processors cannot read from a cache larger than 1 MB
D. A huge cache would slow down the operating system only

**8. (Short answer)** In 1-2 sentences, explain why a laptop with a
bigger cache can finish the same task faster than one with a smaller
cache, even at the same clock speed. Use at least one key word from this
week (hit rate, AMAT, or locality).
